# Shared Reasoning — Reconstruction Validation

Use this document after:

- loading an established project into a new conversation;
- handing work over between collaborators;
- or creating and bootstrapping a fresh project.

The purpose is not to test factual memory.

The purpose is to verify that the collaboration method, project state,
knowledge architecture, and relevant domain model have been reconstructed
well enough for productive work to continue.

For a fresh project, some sections may legitimately contain little material.
In that case, identify what has not yet been established rather than inventing
knowledge to satisfy the test.

Do not guess.

If uncertainty remains after reading the artefacts, classify it explicitly.

## Part 1 — Collaboration Model

Briefly describe:

the purpose of the collaboration protocol;
the distinction between:
accepted knowledge,
historical reasoning,
generators,
anchors,
boundaries,
open questions;
how new accepted knowledge should enter the repository.

## Part 2 — Repository Architecture

Describe the repository architecture.

Explain the purpose of:

common/
project state
session log
domain generators
anchors
reference programs

Explain why these artefacts are separated.

## Part 3 — Concept Graph

Without listing every file, describe the conceptual graph.

Identify:

the central generators;
neighbouring generators;
which concepts appear foundational;
which capsules primarily constrain inference.

Do not merely repeat filenames.

Describe conceptual relationships.

## Part 4 — Reconstruction Test

Choose one concept that is not explicitly explained in one capsule but can be reconstructed from several.

Demonstrate the reconstruction.

State which generators were used.

If anchors influenced the answer, explain how.

## Part 5 — Boundary Test

Identify three boundaries currently present in the project.

For each:

explain why the boundary exists;
describe what incorrect inference it prevents.

## Part 6 — Anchor Test

Choose two anchors.

Explain:

why they are anchors;
what nearby inference drift they prevent.

## Part 7 — Rosetta Test

Describe the role of the reference program.

Do not summarize the program.

Explain why it exists within the knowledge architecture.

Give one example of how it supports reconstruction.

## Part 8 — Uncertainty Test

Identify:

one accepted concept;
one open question;
one area where documentation would currently be preferred over inference.

Explain why each belongs in that category.

## Part 9 — Self Assessment

State:

which parts of the repository feel fully reconstructed;
which parts remain uncertain;
which additional capsule(s), if any, you would revisit before beginning new work.

## Part 10 - Provenance Assessment

For each major concept relied upon in your answers, classify its provenance as:

- Verified by inspection
- Reconstructed from related artefacts
- Not reconstructed

Briefly explain why the classification is appropriate.

## Success Criteria

A successful handover is not measured by perfect factual recall.

A successful handover demonstrates that:

the collaboration methodology has been reconstructed;
accepted knowledge is distinguished from historical reasoning;
generators are used appropriately;
anchors constrain nearby inference;
boundaries are respected;
uncertainty is classified rather than hidden;
conceptual relationships are reconstructed instead of memorized.

If any of these cannot be demonstrated, explain what additional repository material would be required.
